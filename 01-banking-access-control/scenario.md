# Banking Access Control Risk Assessment

## 1. Project Overview

This project presents a simulated cybersecurity risk assessment of employee access controls within a fictional commercial banking environment. The assessment examines how employee access to sensitive banking information and business systems is requested, approved, provisioned, reviewed, modified, and revoked.

The objective is to identify potential weaknesses in identity and access management (IAM), evaluate relevant security controls, assess associated business risks, and recommend practical remediation measures.

## 2. Organization and Business Context

**Organization:** ABC Commercial Bank (Fictional)

ABC Commercial Bank is a fictional financial institution providing retail banking services, including customer account management, deposits, withdrawals, payment processing, and financial transactions.

The bank handles sensitive information, including:

- Customer personally identifiable information (PII).
- Customer account and transaction records.
- Financial and banking information.
- Employee account credentials and access privileges.
- Internal operational and business records.

Protecting this information requires effective access governance to ensure that employees can access only the information and systems necessary to perform their assigned responsibilities.

Inadequate access controls could expose the bank to unauthorized information disclosure, financial fraud, operational disruption, regulatory noncompliance, and reputational damage.

## 3. Assessment Objectives

The assessment aims to:

1. Identify critical information assets and systems requiring access protection.
2. Evaluate employee access privileges and least-privilege practices.
3. Examine access request, approval, and provisioning procedures.
4. Assess periodic user access review practices.
5. Evaluate account deactivation and access revocation following employee termination or role changes.
6. Identify potential access control weaknesses and associated cybersecurity risks.
7. Recommend risk-based security improvements aligned with recognized cybersecurity guidance.

## 4. Assessment Scope

The assessment focuses on the following areas:

| Assessment Area | Description |
|---|---|
| Employee user accounts | Management of employee identities and system accounts. |
| Access privileges | Assignment of permissions based on job responsibilities. |
| Access approvals | Authorization of new access requests and privilege changes. |
| Periodic access reviews | Review of existing permissions to identify excessive or inappropriate access. |
| Employee termination | Timely revocation of access when employment ends. |
| Role changes | Adjustment of access privileges when employees change positions. |
| Access monitoring and logging | Identification and review of potentially unauthorized account activity. |

## 5. Critical Assets

The assessment considers the following categories of information assets:

- Customer information and personally identifiable information.
- Core banking applications and supporting business systems.
- Employee accounts and authentication credentials.
- Financial transaction and account records.
- Access control policies and approval records.
- authentication mechanisms

These assets are considered important because unauthorized access could affect customer confidentiality, financial integrity, and banking operations.

## 6. Threat Scenarios

The assessment considers potential scenarios including:

- Employees receiving access privileges beyond their job responsibilities.
- Former employees retaining active accounts after termination.
- Unauthorized privilege changes without appropriate approval.
- Inadequate periodic access reviews allowing excessive permissions to remain active.
- Compromised employee credentials being used to access sensitive banking information.
- Insufficient monitoring or documentation of access-related activities.

These scenarios are used to identify and evaluate potential risks; they are not claims that these incidents have occurred at ABC Commercial Bank.

## 7. Assessment Methodology

The assessment follows a structured, risk-based approach:

1. **Asset Identification:** Identify relevant information assets and their business importance.
2. **Risk Identification:** Identify potential threats, vulnerabilities, and access control weaknesses.
3. **Risk Analysis:** Evaluate likelihood and potential business impact using the project's defined risk-rating methodology.
4. **Control Evaluation:** Examine relevant existing controls and identify potential deficiencies.
5. **Framework Mapping:** Map relevant security practices to applicable NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 outcomes.
6. **Risk Treatment:** Recommend practical remediation measures based on risk severity and business priorities.
7. **Reporting:** Document findings, risk ratings, control gaps, and recommendations.

Likelihood and impact ratings will be assigned using consistent assessment criteria to support repeatable risk prioritization and transparent remediation decisions.

## 8. Assessment Boundaries and Limitations

This assessment is limited to employee identity and access management within the fictional banking environment.

The assessment does not include:

- Penetration testing or exploitation of banking systems.
- Technical vulnerability scanning.
- Physical security assessments.
- Network infrastructure security assessments.
- Actual customer or employee information.
- Testing of live banking applications.

The assessment is a documentation-based educational simulation. No real organizational systems, accounts, or security controls were accessed or tested.

Where supporting evidence is not available, control effectiveness will be treated as unverified rather than automatically assumed to be effective or ineffective.

## 9. Expected Deliverables

The assessment produces the following documentation:

- Asset inventory.
- Cybersecurity risk register.
- Access control gap analysis.
- NIST CSF 2.0 mapping.
- Final banking access control risk assessment report.

## 10. Disclaimer

ABC Commercial Bank is entirely fictional. All organizational details, scenarios, risks, and assessment materials are developed for educational and professional development purposes. The findings do not represent an actual security assessment or confidential information belonging to any financial institution.
